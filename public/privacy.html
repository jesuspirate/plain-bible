Privacy

My Quiet Bible has no ads, no cookies and no tracking.

This applies to the website and to the iPhone and iPad app alike: the app shows this same website in its own window, and adds nothing of its own.

On your device

Your settings, highlights, bookmarks and recent searches are stored on your device only. Searching happens on your device too.

Counting how many use it

Once a day, the app sends one small “hello” to our sync service, so we know how many devices use it. It carries no account, no id and no cookie. It holds a few yes/no answers (is this the device's first hello, its first this week, its first this month, and is the app on its Home Screen), the app's language, which Bible it opens in, what kind of device it is (iPhone or Mac app, Android app, Fedi, or a browser), the hour and weekday it was first opened that day, whether it still opens a week, a month or three months after its first hello, and how many prayers were recorded, chapters read, verses shared and bookmarks saved on it since the last hello. These are only numbers: never which prayer, chapter, verse or bookmark, and never what a prayer says. The service adds them to a few daily, weekly and monthly counters and keeps nothing else, not even your IP address. We can't tell one device from another, or who anyone is.

Optional sync

If you choose to sign in, the app stores one document for your account on our sync service (hosted by Cloudflare): your highlights, bookmarks and settings, so they match on all your devices. Sign-in uses a passkey or a code we email you. For a passkey we keep its public key. For email we keep only a fingerprint of your address (so we can find your account again) and a hint like “j•••@gmail.com”, never the address itself. The code is sent through our email provider, Resend. We never keep a password or your name. Spoken prayers sync only if you turn that on, and are encrypted on your device first with a key we never see. You can delete your account and everything stored with it at any time from Settings → Account and sync.

Circles

If you join a circle, what you choose to share there (verses, bookmarks, notes, prayers and replies) is encrypted on your device with the circle's key, which only its members' devices hold. The sync service stores it without being able to read it. It does know which accounts are in a circle, when something is shared, and how large it is. Leaving a circle, or deleting your account, removes what you shared there.

Linking another device

When you use Link another device, the app packs a sign-in for your account (and your prayer key, if your prayers sync), encrypts it on your device, and leaves it on the sync service for up to an hour for the other device to pick up. The key is only in the link, so the service can't read it.

Downloads

Bible versions download from this website. Like any website, the hosting service (GitHub Pages) briefly sees your IP address when you download a file. We don't log or keep it.

Recorded readings

When you listen to a Bible that has a recorded reading (the Berean Standard Bible, the King James Version), the recording of the chapter plays straight from its publisher's website, openbible.com. Like any website, it sees your IP address and which chapter was asked for. Nothing else is sent: no account, no id. Reading without listening, or listening with the phone's own voice, asks that website for nothing.

Reporting a problem or sending a suggestion

Settings → Report a problem lets anyone write to us. What you write is sent to us by email and is not stored by the sync service. With it go the app's version, the kind of device (for example "iPhone browser") and the app's language. A problem report also brings the screen you were on, its size and theme, the steps you recorded if you used Show what happens (described in words, such as which button you tapped; no picture is taken and nothing you type is kept), and the last few errors the app ran into since it opened, so we can find the cause. Anything private in the app's address is left out first: a circle invite's or device link's key, a code shown on screen, and the words of a search. Your email address goes with it only if you type it, so we can answer you. To stop the box being misused, the service keeps a scrambled fingerprint of the network address for one day (never the address itself), only to limit how many messages come from one place.
